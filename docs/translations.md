# Translations

After the welcome screen (Start Install or Just try), the configurator asks for a language first, on the screen that carries the intro and the hidden Ctrl+C entry into preparing the machine for another owner. It offers every language in Omarchy's registry (`default/i18n/locales.json` in the settings package). A language with a catalog in `configs/airootfs/usr/share/omarchy-iso/i18n/` also translates the installer. The others are listed by their English name, the installer stays in English, and the language still goes to the installed system as `/etc/omarchy/language`. The keyboard list then preselects the language's layout from `language-defaults.json`. The timezone list preselects the network's guess, and when that fails, the capital of the language's country from the same file. English, Arabic, Swahili and Tamil have no single home country, so they fall back to UTC when the network guess fails.

The installer runs inside kmscon, which draws the gum screens with fontconfig: JetBrains Mono (Omarchy's terminal font), and the bundled Noto Sans Mono CJK for Chinese, Japanese and Korean, with Han characters in the picked language's forms. `.automated_script.sh` starts kmscon on tty1 and runs itself again inside it, and the whole install, the dashboard included, stays there. The launcher is the runtime's own (`install/provisioning/kmscon.sh` in omarchy), vendored at build time like the setup form, so the installer and first-boot owner setup are drawn and typed in alike.

kmscon reads keys through XKB rather than the console keymap and takes its layout and font once, at its start: when the keyboard screen picks another layout, or the language another Han form, the configurator saves the answers so far and exits 75, and `.automated_script.sh` starts kmscon again from the step after the keyboard. The saved answers hold the password, so `kmscon-session.sh` writes them (mode 600) only while the tty1 run is waiting to start kmscon again, and that run removes them however it ends; a configurator run by hand on another VT keeps its kmscon and saves nothing. A kmscon that dies under the installer is started again, from the saved answers if they were not taken yet, otherwise from the first screen, for as long as it keeps dying: nothing else draws the installer.

zh-CN, zh-TW, ja and ko have CJK catalogs so far. Arabic, Hebrew, the Indic scripts, Thai and Vietnamese stay in English. The plain console draws only OEM and cidata installs, which ask nothing. The kernel's own console fonts carry just the IBM PC character set, so there the installer loads Terminus, patched by `console-font.py` with the half blocks the logo is drawn with; it covers Latin, Greek and Cyrillic, and Chinese, Japanese and Korean stay in English.

To add a string, put it in `en.json` and print it with `t <key> [args]`, where `%1`, `%2` take the arguments. A catalog that lacks the key shows it in English until a translation lands. `test/unit/test_installer_i18n.py` fails on a key English does not have, on placeholders that differ from English, and, outside the kmscon languages, on a character the patched font cannot draw. The setup form vendored from Omarchy translates its own strings from Omarchy's catalogs, in the language the configurator names in `OMARCHY_FORM_LANGUAGE`, and takes the keyboard default and the offline timezone through `OMARCHY_FORM_KEYBOARD_DEFAULT` and `OMARCHY_FORM_TIMEZONE_FALLBACK`.
